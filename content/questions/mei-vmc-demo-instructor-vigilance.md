### Question

During a V<sub>MC</sub> demonstration, what are you watching for that the student probably is not?

### Answer

**The stall.** The student is focused on directional control, often to the extent that impending stall indications go unnoticed.

- Spin awareness should be at its greatest during V<sub>MC</sub> demonstrations, stall practice, slow flight, and any condition of high asymmetric power.
- In a multiengine airplane the yawing moment that turns a stall into a spin is already there, supplied by asymmetric thrust.
- A forward CG gives better stall and spin avoidance characteristics but does not eliminate the hazard.

So your attention is split differently from the student's: they own the rudder and the heading, you own the stall margin. If the stall warning or buffet arrives first, that is the recovery cue, and recognizing it is part of the lesson rather than a failed demonstration.

Very few twins have ever been spin-tested, and none are required to be.
