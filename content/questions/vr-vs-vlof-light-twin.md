### Question

What rotation and liftoff speeds should you use in a light twin, and why not rotate as early as possible?

### Answer

- Use the AFM's published rotation speed. Where the AFM permits a choice, the common technique is to rotate at or above **Vmc** and to be climbing at **Vyse** as quickly as practical.
- Rotating early puts you airborne below Vmc. If an engine fails there, you have no ability to maintain directional control, and the airplane will roll toward the dead engine with the ground very close.
- Accelerating on the runway costs a little distance but means that by the time you are airborne, an engine failure is a controllable event.

This is the practical reason many light twin takeoff briefings are built around a hard rule: still on the runway below Vmc means abort.
