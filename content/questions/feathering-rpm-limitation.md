### Question

Why can't you feather the propeller after the engine has stopped turning?

### Answer

Most light twins have **antifeathering latches** (spring-loaded pins) that engage below roughly **800-1,000 RPM**.

- Their purpose is to keep the propeller from feathering every time you shut down on the ramp, which would make the next start difficult and put a large load on the starter.
- The consequence is that once a failed engine has spun down below that RPM, the latches engage and **you can no longer feather it**.

The practical rule: **feather promptly.** If you spend a long time troubleshooting while the propeller winds down, you may lose the option entirely and be stuck with a windmilling propeller for the rest of the flight. Exact RPM figures are airplane-specific - know your AFM's number.
