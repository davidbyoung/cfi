### Question

How do you manage rudder pressure and trim with an engine inoperative?

### Answer

- Apply rudder immediately and firmly. The initial input is large - stopping the yaw is the first priority, before anything else.
- Once the airplane is stabilized in zero sideslip, **trim the rudder** to relieve the pressure. Holding a heavy leg load for a long single-engine approach is how mistakes creep in.
- Retrim as airspeed and power change, since the required rudder varies with both.
- Be ready to **remove** that trim during recovery or a change in configuration; rudder trim held into a power reduction can produce yaw in the opposite direction.

The workload argument matters: an untrimmed leg gets tired, and a tired leg relaxes at exactly the wrong moment on short final.
