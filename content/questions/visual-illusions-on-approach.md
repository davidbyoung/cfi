### Question

What visual illusions can bite you on an instrument approach or a night landing?

### Answer

- **Runway width** - A narrow runway makes you feel high, so you fly low. A wide runway makes you feel low.
- **Runway or terrain slope** - An upsloping runway or rising terrain makes you feel high. Downsloping does the opposite.
- **Featureless terrain (black hole)** - Approaching over dark water or unlit terrain with no visual cues makes you feel high, and pilots fly dangerously low approaches as a result.
- **Haze** - Makes the runway look farther away and makes you feel higher, so you descend early. Unusually clear air does the reverse.
- **Rain on the windshield** - Refraction makes the runway appear lower than it is, so you fly low.
- **False horizon** - Sloping cloud tops, a dark night with scattered ground lights and stars, or a lighted shoreline mistaken for the horizon.
- **Autokinesis** - Stare at a single light against a dark background long enough and it appears to move.

Defenses: brief the TDZE and the VGSI, fly the published or advisory vertical guidance all the way down, cross-check altitude against distance, and keep your scan going instead of staring at the runway.
