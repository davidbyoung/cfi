### Question

What does power on the operating engine do to V<sub>MC</sub>?

### Answer

**More power raises V<sub>MC</sub>.** It is the most direct relationship of any factor: the yaw you are fighting comes from thrust on one side, so more thrust means a larger yawing moment and a higher speed needed to generate the rudder force that opposes it.

- Certification uses **maximum available takeoff power initially on each engine** - [§ 23.149(b)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2014-title14-vol1/pdf/CFR-2014-title14-vol1-sec23-149.pdf) - because that is the worst case.
- The rule also requires that it **not be necessary to reduce power** on the operating engine to keep control at V<sub>MC</sub> - (e). Throttling back is not an allowed part of the test.
- It follows that **reducing power on the good engine removes the yaw at its source** - which is why power comes off, not on, in a V<sub>MC</sub> recovery.
- This is also the mechanism behind the altitude effect: a normally aspirated engine cannot make takeoff power up high, so the asymmetric thrust weakens and V<sub>MC</sub> falls.

The corollary is worth saying out loud: less power means **less** asymmetric thrust, so a tired or partially failed operating engine leaves you with a **lower** V<sub>MC</sub>, not a higher one.
