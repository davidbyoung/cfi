### Question

What happens to the propeller if you lose engine oil pressure?

### Answer

On a typical light twin with counterweighted, feathering propellers, **the propeller moves toward feather**.

- Oil pressure is what holds the blades out of feather. Lose it, and the counterweights and spring take over.
- This is the opposite of most single-engine airplanes, where oil pressure drives the blades toward **high** pitch and a loss of pressure sends the propeller to **low pitch** (high RPM).

The design choice makes sense for a twin: a failed engine that automatically feathers is a far better outcome than one that windmills flat and produces maximum drag.
