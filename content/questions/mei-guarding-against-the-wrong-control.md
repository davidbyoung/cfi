### Question

How do you keep a student from securing the wrong engine or standing on the wrong rudder?

### Answer

The designed defense is procedural, and the handbook names one concrete backup.

- **The verify step is the guard.** Retarding the throttle of the engine believed to have failed is a reversible test before an irreversible action, and teaching the student to never skip it does more than any physical intervention.
- **On the takeoff roll,** if the student does not promptly retard both throttles, the instructor can always pull the other mixture.
- **Brief the division of controls before the flight,** and restate it in the air. Ambiguity about who is operating which systems or controls increases the likelihood of an unintended outcome.

Beyond that, instructors commonly shadow the quadrant with a hand and stay ready to block a pedal before a wrong input loads up. That is accepted practice rather than published procedure, so treat it as technique and brief it, not as something the student should be surprised by.
