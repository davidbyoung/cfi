### Question

What vestibular illusions should you expect in IMC, and what causes each?

### Answer

- **The leans** - The most common one. A slow roll you don't feel, corrected abruptly, leaves you feeling banked the other way while the instruments show wings level.
- **Coriolis** - Moving your head on a different axis during a prolonged turn stirs the fluid in the semicircular canals and produces a violent sensation of tumbling. This is why you don't drop your head to grab a chart in a turn.
- **Graveyard spiral** - In a prolonged coordinated turn, the sensation of turning fades. Roll out and you feel like you're turning the other way, so you turn back, notice the altitude loss, and pull, which tightens the spiral.
- **Somatogravic** - Rapid acceleration feels like a pitch up, which tempts you to push over. That's dangerous on a go-around or missed approach. Deceleration feels like a pitch down.
- **Inversion** - An abrupt level off after a climb feels like tumbling backwards.
- **Elevator** - An updraft feels like a climb, prompting a nose down input.

The fix for all of them is the same: believe the instruments, keep the scan moving, and keep your head still.
