### Question

What happens to the magnetic compass when you accelerate, and why? What about when you're turning to the north - what magnetic compass error do you have to anticipate, and what causes it?

### Answer

- **ANDS** (Accelerate North, Decelerate South): On east/west headings, acceleration causes a false turn toward north and deceleration toward south because the compass is weighted on its south-seeking end to counter magnetic dip. Inertia makes that weighted end lag or swing forward, rotating the compass card.
- **UNOS** (Undershoot North, Overshoot South): When turning toward north, the compass lags, so undershoot north. When turning toward south, it leads, so overshoot south. This happens because the compass hangs pendulously below its pivot, and banking lets magnetic dip tilt and rotate the compass assembly, producing the turning error.
