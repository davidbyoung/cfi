### Question

Your Vmc sources cite § 23.149, but that section isn't in the current regulations. Is this material out of date?

### Answer

**No.** An airplane's **certification basis is fixed at the time of type certification**. The 2017 rewrite of part 23 is not retroactive and changed nothing about any existing airplane's Vmc. The red line on a Seminole, Seneca, Duchess, or Baron was determined under the legacy rule, so the legacy conditions are the ones that literally describe the airplane you fly. The AFH and the ACS still teach them.

What actually changed:

- The 2017 rewrite replaced part 23's prescriptive airworthiness standards with performance-based ones. **§ 23.149 no longer exists** in the current CFR, which is why sources cite it by its last published edition.
- Its replacement, **§ 23.2135 (Controllability)**, still defines the speed - **§ 23.2135(c)**: "VMC is the calibrated airspeed at which, following the sudden critical loss of thrust, it is possible to maintain control of the airplane. For multiengine airplanes, the applicant must determine VMC, if applicable, for the most critical configurations used in takeoff and landing operations."
- What it no longer does is **state the numbers**. There is no 5° bank, no 150 lb rudder force, no 20° heading change, and no 1.2 Vs1 cap in the rule text. Those now live in the **accepted means of compliance** (industry consensus standards) an applicant uses to show compliance, rather than in the CFR itself.

So quote the § 23.149 conditions with confidence. If it comes up, be able to add that they come from the version of part 23 your airplane was certified under rather than from today's CFR.
