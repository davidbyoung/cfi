### Question

You earned your instrument rating in a single-engine airplane and then add AMEL. Can you file IFR in the twin?

### Answer

Only if you demonstrated instrument proficiency in a multiengine airplane on the checkride.

- The instrument rating itself is issued for the **category** (airplane), not the class, so nothing about the rating stops you.
- But per the ACS: if you hold both a single- and multiengine rating and have **not** demonstrated instrument proficiency in a multiengine airplane, your certificate must bear a limitation that multiengine flight is permitted in **VFR conditions only**.
- That's what ACS Tasks X.C and X.D exist for. They aren't required if you already demonstrated instrument proficiency in a multi, or if you don't hold an instrument airplane rating at all.
- Removing the limitation later requires testing those Tasks in an airplane with a manufacturer's published Vmc.
- **Currency is a separate question from privileges.** Instrument currency under [§ 61.57(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.57#p-61.57%28c%29) is by category, so approaches flown in a single count toward currency for the multi and vice versa. That does **not** lift a VFR-only limitation - if your certificate carries one, being instrument current does not authorize you to fly the twin IFR.
