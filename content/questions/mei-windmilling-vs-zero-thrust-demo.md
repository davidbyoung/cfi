### Question

Why spend flight time showing the difference between a windmilling and a simulated feathered propeller?

### Answer

Because the windmilling propeller lies to the pilot, and the lie is expensive.

- A windmilling propeller has often given the improperly trained multiengine pilot **the mistaken perception that the engine is still developing useful thrust**.
- That produces a **psychological reluctance to feather**, since feathering stops the propeller turning and feels like giving something up.
- The reluctance costs exactly the performance the airplane needed.

So demonstrate both, back to back, at altitude: the same airplane at the same speed and weight, windmilling and then at zero thrust. The rate of climb difference makes the argument better than any briefing.

A competent instructor teaches the student the critical importance of feathering **in a timely manner** if a real failure ever happens. This demonstration is how that lesson stops being a number in a book.
