### Question

What is fuel crossfeed for, and what are its limitations?

### Answer

**It lets an operating engine draw fuel from the tank on the opposite side.**

- On most twins it's an **emergency procedure**: extend range and endurance in single-engine flight, and keep the airplane **laterally balanced** on a long OEI leg. A few models permit it as a normal fuel-balancing technique - your AFM says which kind you have.
- If a suitable airport is close by, there's no need to consider it at all.
- **Never for takeoff**, or for a normal landing with both engines running. The one exception: a single-engine landing on crossfeed, when returning the operating engine to its normal source would starve it.
- Otherwise terminate crossfeed before landing and return the operating engine to its **main tank**.
- Procedures are highly airplane-specific - which selector, which boost pump, in what order. Getting it wrong can starve the running engine.

Treat it as a checklist item, never a memory item. It also isn't urgent: get the airplane cleaned up, stabilized, and headed somewhere before reconfiguring fuel.
