### Question

Can you fail an engine without warning the student?

### Answer

Only in the right context, and only with prior agreement.

- **Brief simulated failures in advance.** The student should know how a failure will be introduced and what response is expected of them.
- **Unannounced failures are suitable only in testing and checking scenarios**, and only when both pilots have agreed to that before the flight.
- A pre-flight brief along the lines of "at some point today I will fail an engine with no further warning" satisfies that. Quietly deciding mid-lesson to catch someone out does not.

Surprise is a checking tool rather than a teaching one. During instruction the student is building a procedure, and nobody practices a procedure well while being startled by it. Save it for the stage check, and brief it even then.
