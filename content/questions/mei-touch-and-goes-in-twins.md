### Question

Are touch-and-goes appropriate in a light twin?

### Answer

The handbook calls the practice **"always somewhat controversial"** and is cautious about it.

- The learning value may be offset by the hazard of reconfiguring the airplane in extremely limited time, plus the loss of the follow-through you get from a full stop.
- They are **not recommended during initial aircraft familiarization**.
- **Solo touch-and-goes in twins are strongly discouraged.**
- A **full stop with a taxi back** is preferable while the student is still new to the airplane.

If you do them, brief the division of labor beforehand and again in the flare: the student ordinarily maintains directional control with the left hand on the yoke and the right on the throttles, while the instructor resets flaps and trim and **announces when the airplane is reconfigured**.

Allow for the runway, too. A multiengine airplane uses considerably more of it for a touch-and-go than a single does.
