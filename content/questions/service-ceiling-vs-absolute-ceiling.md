### Question

What's the difference between a service ceiling and an absolute ceiling?

### Answer

- **Service ceiling** - The highest density altitude at which the airplane can maintain a steady **100 fpm** rate of climb with **both engines operating**, clean, at best rate of climb speed.
- **Absolute ceiling** - The density altitude at which the airplane can no longer climb at all. Rate of climb is **zero**, and V<sub>X</sub> and V<sub>Y</sub> have converged to a single speed.

The single-engine versions use the same idea with one engine inoperative and its propeller feathered, but **the service-ceiling climb rate is different**: the **single-engine service ceiling** is where the airplane can no longer hold **50 fpm** OEI, and the **single-engine absolute ceiling** is where OEI climb stops altogether. Above that absolute ceiling, the AFH says V<sub>YSE</sub> gives the minimum rate of sink.

The 100/50 split is a favorite oral question, and swapping the two numbers is the usual way to miss it.
