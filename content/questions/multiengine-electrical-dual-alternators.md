### Question

How does a twin's electrical system differ from a single's?

### Answer

- Two engines means **two alternators (or generators)** feeding a common bus, usually through separate voltage regulators and with individual field switches and circuit breakers.
- Normal operation shares the load. Lose one alternator and the remaining one typically carries the **entire** load, though possibly with reduced margin.
- Many twins have a **split bus** or tie relay so that a fault on one side can be isolated.
- There may be **two batteries**, or one battery with a more complex bus structure.

For the oral, be ready to explain what indication you'd get for a single alternator failure, what load shedding you'd do, and whether your airplane can support the full electrical load - including known-ice and avionics - on one alternator.
