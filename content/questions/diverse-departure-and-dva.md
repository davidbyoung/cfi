### Question

If the airport has no published departure procedure, what obstacle protection do you actually have?

### Answer

- If nothing penetrates the **40:1 obstacle identification surface**, the runway passes what's called a diverse departure assessment and no ODP is published. That means a normal climb protects you in any direction off that runway.
- That protection assumes you:
  - Cross the departure end of the runway at least **35 ft** above the departure end elevation
  - Climb to **400 ft** above the departure end elevation before making the initial turn
  - Maintain at least **200 ft/NM** until the minimum IFR altitude
- A **Diverse Vector Area (DVA)** is airspace the facility has evaluated so that ATC can vector you off the departure below the minimum vectoring altitude. A DVA may carry its own required climb gradient, and it's published in the takeoff minimums section of the TPP.
- None of this replaces looking at the terrain yourself. The assessment stops where the evaluation area ends, and a steeper than standard climb gradient may be published instead of a departure route.
