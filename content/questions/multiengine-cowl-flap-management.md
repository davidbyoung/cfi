### Question

What do cowl flaps do, and how do you manage them normally and with an engine out?

### Answer

They meter cooling air through the engine compartment. Open buys cooling and costs drag, so the schedule tracks where cooling demand is high and airspeed is low.

- **Ground operations: fully open**, unless the AFM/POH directs otherwise.
- **Takeoff and climb: open.** High power and low airspeed is the worst cooling case there is.
- **Level off: closed**, once cylinder head and oil temperatures are in their normal ranges. Lean the mixtures after the temperatures stabilize.
- **Engine out, failed engine: closed.** Of everything on the securing checklist, this is the **only item that costs climb performance** if you leave it undone.
- **Engine out, operating engine: whatever the temperatures require.** It is at high power and low airspeed with a long way to go, and cowl flaps are temperature control before they are a cleanup item.

That last pair is the same asymmetry as the rest of single-engine flight: clean up everything on the dead side, and keep the live engine alive even when it costs you drag.
