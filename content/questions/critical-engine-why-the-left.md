### Question

Walk me through why losing the left engine is worse than losing the right.

### Answer

Think of it as thrust arms about the center of gravity.

- Because of P-factor, each engine's effective thrust line sits slightly to the **right** of its physical centerline.
- If the **right** engine fails, the operating **left** engine's thrust line has been shifted **toward** the fuselage - a **shorter** arm - so it produces a **smaller** yawing moment. Easier to control, lower V<sub>MC</sub>.
- If the **left** engine fails, the operating **right** engine's thrust line has been shifted **away** from the fuselage - a **longer** arm - so it produces a **larger** yawing moment. Harder to control, higher V<sub>MC</sub>.

Since losing the left engine leaves you fighting the bigger moment, the **left engine is critical**. Spiraling slipstream, accelerated slipstream, and torque all reinforce the same conclusion.
