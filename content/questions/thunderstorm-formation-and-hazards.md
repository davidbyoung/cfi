### Question

What does a thunderstorm need to form, what are its stages, and what hazards does it present?

### Answer

**Three ingredients**

- Sufficient moisture
- An unstable lapse rate
- A lifting action (a front, terrain, surface heating, or convergence)

**Three stages**

| Stage       | What's happening                                                                                    |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cumulus     | Updrafts build the cell, with no precipitation reaching the surface yet                             |
| Mature      | Precipitation begins falling, so updrafts and downdrafts coexist. Every hazard is at its worst here |
| Dissipating | Downdrafts dominate and the cell rains itself out                                                   |

**Hazards**

Severe turbulence, wind shear and microbursts, hail (which can be thrown miles outside the cloud), severe icing, lightning, heavy rain, tornadoes, and a complete loss of visibility.

**What it means for us**

**Never fly under a cell**, since that's where the shear and hail live. Avoid anything severe or with an intense echo by **at least 20 NM**. Circumnavigate areas of heavy coverage rather than picking through them. And remember that an embedded cell in IMC gives you no visual warning at all.
