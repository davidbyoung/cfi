### Question

Beyond startling the student, why do simulated failures have to be introduced smoothly?

### Answer

**Dynamic crankshaft counterweights.** Abrupt throttle reductions are one of the things that damage them.

- The other named offenders are **high rpm with low manifold pressure**, **over-boosting**, and **propeller feathering**.
- Severe or repeated abuse of counterweights eventually leads to **engine failure**.
- They are found on larger, more complex engines. Check with maintenance personnel or the engine manufacturer to find out whether the engines you instruct in have them.

This is worth knowing because the instructor is the one repeatedly yanking throttles for a living. A single abrupt reduction is unlikely to break anything; a habit of them, several times a flight, several flights a week, is a different exposure.
