### Question

Density altitude is high and V<sub>MC</sub> is at or below the stall. How do you still demonstrate V<sub>MC</sub>?

### Answer

By **artificially limiting rudder travel** to simulate maximum available rudder. The handbook sanctions this as a training technique for exactly this case, and also calls it a rudder blocking technique. In the case that you are simulating a left engine inoperative, the instructor can block some movement of the left rudder pedal in the direction towards the pilot (and the right rudder pedal with a simulated right engine inoperative).

- Limiting travel means "full rudder" arrives sooner, so the airplane departs directionally at a **higher airspeed**, above the stall, where you can show the real cue.
- Use a speed **well above V<sub>S</sub>, approximately 20 knots**, when limiting rudder travel.
- It **avoids the hazard of spinning** from a stall with high asymmetric power, and it still demonstrates loss of directional control.

The alternative is not "demonstrate it anyway." An actual V<sub>MC</sub> demonstration may simply not be possible at some density altitudes, or in an airplane whose V<sub>MC</sub> is equal to or below V<sub>S</sub>, and riding one down to a stall with the good engine at takeoff power is how a demonstration becomes a spin.
