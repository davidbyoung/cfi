### Question

If you lose one of two engines, how much performance have you actually lost?

### Answer

**Roughly 80% of your climb performance, not 50%.**

- You lose half your _power_, but climb performance depends on **excess** power - what's left over after overcoming drag. Most of the remaining engine's output is consumed just holding level flight.
- On top of that, the dead engine adds drag, and the control inputs needed to stop the yaw add more.
- A typical light twin at gross weight on a warm day may climb only **100-200 fpm** on one engine, and some can't climb at all.

The practical conclusion: a light twin's second engine buys you time and options, not guaranteed climb. Treat single-engine climb as something you verify in the charts before takeoff, not something you assume.
