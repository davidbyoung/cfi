### Question

An engine fails just after liftoff, below V<sub>YSE</sub>, with runway remaining. What now?

### Answer

If there is usable runway ahead, **land on it.** Reduce power on the operating engine to reduce the yawing moment, lower the nose, and put the airplane back down.

- Below V<sub>YSE</sub> with the gear down and a windmilling propeller, a light twin usually cannot climb. Attempting to continue trades a landing you can make for a crash you cannot avoid.
- Retracting the gear to climb commits you before you know whether the airplane will climb at all.
- If you are airborne with **no** runway remaining and cannot climb, the decision becomes a controlled off-airport landing. Close the throttles first - with the asymmetry gone you can fly the airplane wings level - then land roughly straight ahead in the best area available. Slowing toward minimum speed while still carrying power on one side is how a forced landing turns into a V<sub>MC</sub> rollover.

This is precisely why the takeoff briefing exists. The decision is made on the ground, not in three seconds at 50 feet.
