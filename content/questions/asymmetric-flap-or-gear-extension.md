### Question

What happens if the flaps or gear extend asymmetrically?

### Answer

- **Asymmetric flaps** produce a strong **rolling moment** toward the retracted side. The response is to **stop moving the flaps immediately** - leave them where they are - and use aileron and rudder to hold the wings level.
  - Do **not** try to fix it by cycling the flaps.
  - Expect a **higher approach speed** and a longer landing, and plan a long, stable final. Consider that roll authority may run out at low airspeed, which sets a hard floor on approach speed.
- **Asymmetric gear** is primarily a landing problem - the airplane will want to swerve toward the extended side at touchdown. Try the emergency extension procedure, and if one main won't lock, plan the landing accordingly.

Both cases share a theme: stop making it worse, fly the airplane, and land somewhere long and into the wind.
