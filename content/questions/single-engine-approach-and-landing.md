### Question

How do you fly a single-engine approach and landing?

### Answer

It is **essentially a normal approach and landing** - same pattern, same altitudes, same key positions. What changes is that there is less power and it's asymmetric, so the operating engine carries a higher-than-normal setting.

- **V<sub>YSE</sub> is the minimum airspeed** all the way around. Hold it until the landing is assured, then slow to **1.3 V<sub>S0</sub> or the AFM speed**. Don't add speed of your own; it only lengthens the roll.
- **Gear down on downwind if performance allows**, confirmed no later than **abeam the intended landing point**. Flaps typically 10° downwind, 25° on base.
- **Let performance delay the flaps**, not a rule. If speed decays or the sink rate builds, wait until closer in. A partial-flap landing is fine.
- Fly a **normal 3° glidepath**. Slightly steeper is acceptable; the **long, flat, low approach** is the one to avoid.
- Keep **zero sideslip** until you transition to land, and make power changes **smoothly** - a large, sudden application at low speed is a V<sub>MC</sub> problem.
- **Treat it as committed.** A single-engine go-around is a risk to manage, not a plan.
- Pattern direction doesn't matter: the AFH calls turns toward the failed engine of no consequence **for controllability and performance**.

Late in the flare, as power comes off, the asymmetric yaw goes away, so be ready to release the rudder pressure you have been holding or the airplane will yaw the other way.
