### Question

How do you fly an emergency descent in a twin?

### Answer

- Situations: **depressurization**, **smoke**, or an **engine fire**.
- **Airspeed and configuration come from the POH/AFM**, not from a generic technique. Some call for idle power, gear down, flaps or speedbrakes; others don't use the gear at all, and extending it can be wrong for the airplane or the emergency. Fly the checklist.
- **The bank angle is what the ACS fixes**: **30-45°**, to hold a positive load factor and keep the nose from rising as speed builds.
- Standards: clear the area first, airspeed **+0/-10 kt**, level off **±100 ft**.
- Don't exceed **Vle** with the gear down or **Vlo** in transit. In rough air use the AFM's turbulent-air penetration speed - and remember **Va decreases with weight**.
- Level off at a safe altitude - minimum safe altitude for terrain, or a cabin-safe level after a depressurization.

The bank is the part people forget, and it's the one element that's the same in every airplane.
