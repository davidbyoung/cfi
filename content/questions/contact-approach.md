### Question

What is a contact approach, and what do you need to get one?

### Answer

A contact approach is an approach you can request in lieu of flying a published instrument approach, while remaining on your IFR clearance.

To get one:

- You have to request it. ATC cannot offer it to you.
- Reported ground visibility at the destination must be at least **1 SM**.
- You must be **clear of clouds** with at least **1 SM flight visibility**, and be able to reasonably expect to continue to the destination in those conditions.
- The destination must have a published standard or special instrument approach procedure.

Once you accept it, you're responsible for obstruction clearance and for navigating to the airport. ATC still provides separation from other IFR and special VFR traffic.

Compared to a visual approach, there's no 1,000 ft ceiling and 3 SM visibility requirement, and you don't need the airport in sight to be cleared, but there are also no published minimums protecting you.
