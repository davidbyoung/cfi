### Question

What are the four factors that determine the critical engine?

### Answer

Remember them as **PAST**:

- **P**-factor - At a positive angle of attack the descending blade produces more thrust. With both propellers turning clockwise, the descending blade of each is on its right side, so each engine's effective thrust line is displaced to the right. That gives the **right** engine a longer arm from the centerline.
- **A**ccelerated slipstream - The high-velocity air behind each propeller produces extra lift on the wing section behind it. Because the thrust line is displaced right, the center of that added lift is farther from the fuselage on the right engine, producing a longer rolling arm.
- **S**piraling slipstream - The corkscrew of air off the left propeller wraps around the fuselage and strikes the **left** side of the vertical stabilizer, helping counter the yaw when the right engine fails. Lose the left engine and you lose that help.
- **T**orque - Both engines turning clockwise produce a rolling moment to the left. With the left engine failed, the right engine's torque rolls the airplane further toward the dead engine, adding to the problem.

All four point the same way: losing the **left** engine on a conventional twin with clockwise-rotating props is worse.
