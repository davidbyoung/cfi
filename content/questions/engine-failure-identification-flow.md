### Question

How do you identify which engine failed? Why can't you just read the gauges?

### Answer

**Dead foot, dead engine.**

- The airplane yaws toward the failed engine, so you push the rudder on the **operating** side to stop it. The foot that is doing nothing is on the side of the **dead** engine.
- This works because it keys off a control input you are already making, not off an instrument you have to find and interpret.

Why not just read the gauges? On a sudden failure, engine instruments can be ambiguous or slow - a partial power loss may show near-normal indications, and manifold pressure can read high on a failed engine. The rudder never lies about which way the airplane is yawing.

Confirm with instruments once the airplane is under control, but identify with your feet first.
