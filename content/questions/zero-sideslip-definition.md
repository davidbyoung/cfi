### Question

What is zero sideslip?

### Answer

**The combination of bank and rudder that makes the airplane fly straight through the air with the fuselage aligned to the relative wind, producing the least possible drag with one engine inoperative.**

- With asymmetric thrust, you need rudder to stop the yaw. Rudder alone leaves the airplane flying slightly sideways through the air, which is drag you cannot afford.
- Adding a small bank toward the operating engine lets the horizontal component of lift do part of the work, so less rudder is needed and the fuselage lines up with the airflow.
- It is the configuration in which published single-engine climb performance was actually measured. Fly it any other way and you will not see book numbers.
