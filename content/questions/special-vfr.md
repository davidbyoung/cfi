### Question

What is special VFR? When would you use it? How do you request it? Are there any rules you must follow while in special VFR?

### Answer

**What it is**

Special VFR (SVFR) is an ATC clearance that allows a VFR aircraft to operate within controlled airspace designated to the surface for an airport when the weather is below basic VFR minimums. For airplanes, SVFR generally requires at least 1 statute mile visibility and clear of clouds.

**When you'd use it**

You might use SVFR to enter, leave, or operate within a surface area when the weather is below normal VFR minimums, but conditions are still good enough to safely remain clear of clouds with the required visibility. For example, an airport might have a low cloud layer that makes the field technically IFR, while visibility below the layer is adequate for a short VFR departure or arrival.

**How to request it**

To request it, you can call tower and say "Tower, Archer 3096B, request Special VFR departure to the west".

**Rules to follow**

For airplanes, you need at least 1 SM visibility and must remain clear of clouds. At night, the pilot must be instrument rated and the airplane must be IFR equipped. SVFR is traffic permitting, applies only within the surface area, and is prohibited for fixed-wing aircraft at certain airports.
