### Question

You lose one alternator in a twin. What are you dealing with?

### Answer

Better off than a single, but not a non-event.

- Identify it - a low-voltage light, a zero or negative reading on that side's ammeter, or a split between the two.
- Attempt a **reset** of the alternator field per the checklist; a tripped field breaker sometimes resets.
- If it won't come back, the remaining alternator normally carries the whole load, but **check the load against its rating**. Known-ice equipment, pitot heat, landing lights, and a full avionics stack can exceed what one alternator will supply.
- **Shed nonessential load** proactively rather than waiting to discover the battery is discharging.
- Consider whether to continue. In IMC or at night, a single remaining alternator is a single point of failure, and landing at a suitable airport is usually the right call.

Losing **both** is a battery-endurance problem: shed everything but essentials, and land soon.
