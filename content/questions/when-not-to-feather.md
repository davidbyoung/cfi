### Question

Is there a time you would not feather a failed engine?

### Answer

Yes, in a few cases:

- **Below the AFM's minimum altitude or airspeed for the procedure**, where the manufacturer specifies flying the airplane first.
- **When you are about to land anyway** - Very low, with the runway made, securing the engine is less important than flying the landing.
- **During training**, where instructors commonly simulate a failure with **zero thrust** rather than actually feathering, to keep a restart from being necessary.
- When you **have not verified** which engine failed. Feathering the wrong one is worse than the drag of a windmilling propeller.

Also relevant: the ACS requires that Tasks involving actual feathering or shutdown be done only at a position and altitude where a **safe landing on an established airport** is possible if the propeller won't unfeather or the engine won't restart.
